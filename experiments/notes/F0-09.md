# F0-09 — ACF/PACF and context-window justification

Generated by `scripts/run_acf_pacf.py`. Run on four token sets, not three: ETT's
hourly (`ETTh1/h2`) and 15-minute (`ETTm1/m2`) variants are analysed **separately**
(`ett_hourly`, `ett_15min`), not pooled into one "ett" — see below for why that
mattered. `finance` and `ett_daily` (F0-07's daily-resampled ETT) complete the set.

## Method

A compound token (`C2_V1`) has no single natural total ordering, so it's mapped to
**two** ordinal scales, analysed separately, not collapsed into one artificial
combined number: the change label (0–4) and the volatility label (0–4). ACF uses
**Bartlett's formula** for the confidence bound; PACF uses the conventional
large-sample bound (±1.96/√N) — Bartlett's formula is specifically an ACF result, and
pairing them this way is the standard convention. Lags computed out to 300 — F0-08
already found lag-10 mutual information clearly nonzero (0.49–0.59 bits) in every
domain, so stopping at 10 would not find where autocorrelation actually decays.

**Why ETT is split by sampling frequency here.** A first pass pooled all 4 ETT
variants into one "ett" series, as every earlier task (F0-05–F0-08) had. The
resulting ACF looked periodic, but turned out to be a blend of the hourly variants'
slower decay and the 15-min variants' genuine daily oscillation — an artifact of
concatenating two sampling frequencies where lag *k* means a different amount of
physical time in each segment. This is the same granularity issue F0-07 found for
cross-domain comparison, showing up again here within a single domain. Splitting by
frequency removed the ambiguity.

**No automated "is this genuine decay or coincidental dip" classifier was trusted.**
Two attempts were made and both failed: a loose rule (single later exceedance beyond
2× the bound counts as "re-emergence") flagged nothing as periodic, missing the
15-min series' obvious oscillation; a stricter rule (3 consecutive lags outside the
bound) flagged *every* series, including finance's near-instant clean decorrelation —
with 300 lags tested, even true white noise produces occasional short runs outside a
95% band by chance. The classification below (oscillating vs. clean decay) comes from
directly reading the curves in Figure A8, which is more reliable than a brittle
automatic rule for this specific judgment call.

## Result

| domain | channel | first lag stably inside Bartlett bound | classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| ett_hourly | change | never (300 tested) | **oscillating** — exact 24-lag period |
| ett_hourly | volatility | never (300 tested) | slow/persistent decay, not periodic |
| ett_15min | change | 210 | **oscillating** — exact 96-lag period |
| ett_15min | volatility | 143 | **oscillating** — same ~96-100 lag period |
| finance | change | 10 | clean decay |
| finance | volatility | 137 | clean decay |
| ett_daily | change | 8 | clean decay |
| ett_daily | volatility | 54 | clean decay (noisier — small sample) |

**ETT's oscillation periods are exact 24-hour cycles, not approximate.** Peak-to-peak
spacing measured directly from the ACF curve: `ett_hourly` peaks at lags 24, 48, 72,
96, 120... (spacing exactly 24, every single gap); `ett_15min` peaks at lags 96, 192,
288 (spacing exactly 96 = 96 × 15 min = 24 hours). Both are the same physical
daily cycle, sampled at different resolutions — real, measured evidence for exactly
the "ETT will likely require seasonal handling" expectation from F0-03, and precisely
the pattern the event-token mechanism (`E_HOUR_PEAK` etc.) is designed to capture.
It is not something a longer context window would resolve — a fixed window can shift
where in the cycle it starts, but can't give the model an absolute clock position,
which is what a recurring calendar-relative pattern actually needs.

**Finance and ett_daily decay cleanly to near-zero**, no periodic re-emergence.
`finance`'s change channel decorrelates almost immediately (lag 10); its volatility
channel takes much longer (lag 137), consistent with the well-known persistence of
volatility clustering. `ett_daily` decorrelates faster on both channels (8, 54) —
plausibly because daily resampling (`.last()` of each calendar day) already discards
the sub-daily seasonal structure that dominates the native-frequency series, leaving
less to decorrelate from in the first place.

## Recommended context window: **150** (was 30)

Derived from the **clean-decay** series only — `finance`'s volatility channel (lag
137) is the binding constraint, rounded up to 150 for a small margin. The two
oscillating ETT series are deliberately excluded from this calculation: a fixed
window sized to their ~24-hour period wouldn't actually resolve daily seasonality
(the phase would still depend on where the window happens to start), so sizing the
window around them would inflate context length for no real benefit. That
seasonality is what event tokens exist for, not the window.

**This differs from the previous arbitrary value of 30 by a factor of 5.**
`WORKING_PLAN.md` updated accordingly (see below).
