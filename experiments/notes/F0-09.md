# F0-09 — ACF/PACF and context-window justification

Generated by `scripts/run_acf_pacf.py`. Run on four token sets, not three: ETT's
hourly (`ETTh1/h2`) and 15-minute (`ETTm1/m2`) variants are analysed **separately**
(`ett_hourly`, `ett_15min`), not pooled into one "ett" — see below for why that
mattered. `finance` and `ett_daily` (F0-07's daily-resampled ETT) complete the set.

## Method

A compound token (`C2_V1`) has no single natural total ordering, so it's mapped to
**two** ordinal scales, analysed separately, not collapsed into one artificial
combined number: the change label (0–4) and the volatility label (0–4). ACF uses
**Bartlett's formula** for the confidence bound; PACF uses the conventional
large-sample bound (±1.96/√N) — Bartlett's formula is specifically an ACF result, and
pairing them this way is the standard convention. Lags computed out to 300 — F0-08
already found lag-10 mutual information clearly nonzero (0.49–0.59 bits) in every
domain, so stopping at 10 would not find where autocorrelation actually decays.

**Why ETT is split by sampling frequency here.** A first pass pooled all 4 ETT
variants into one "ett" series, as every earlier task (F0-05–F0-08) had. The
resulting ACF looked periodic, but turned out to be a blend of the hourly variants'
slower decay and the 15-min variants' genuine daily oscillation — an artifact of
concatenating two sampling frequencies where lag *k* means a different amount of
physical time in each segment. This is the same granularity issue F0-07 found for
cross-domain comparison, showing up again here within a single domain. Splitting by
frequency removed the ambiguity.

**No automated "is this genuine decay or coincidental dip" classifier was trusted.**
Two attempts were made and both failed: a loose rule (single later exceedance beyond
2× the bound counts as "re-emergence") flagged nothing as periodic, missing the
15-min series' obvious oscillation; a stricter rule (3 consecutive lags outside the
bound) flagged *every* series, including finance's near-instant clean decorrelation —
with 300 lags tested, even true white noise produces occasional short runs outside a
95% band by chance. The classification below (oscillating vs. clean decay) comes from
directly reading the curves in Figure A8, which is more reliable than a brittle
automatic rule for this specific judgment call.

## Result

| domain | channel | first lag stably inside Bartlett bound | classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| ett_hourly | change | never (300 tested) | **oscillating** — exact 24-lag period |
| ett_hourly | volatility | never (300 tested) | slow/persistent decay, not periodic |
| ett_15min | change | 210 | **oscillating** — exact 96-lag period |
| ett_15min | volatility | 143 | **oscillating** — same ~96-100 lag period |
| finance | change | 10 | clean decay |
| finance | volatility | 137 | clean decay |
| ett_daily | change | 8 | clean decay |
| ett_daily | volatility | 54 | clean decay (noisier — small sample) |

**ETT's oscillation periods are exact 24-hour cycles, not approximate.** Peak-to-peak
spacing measured directly from the ACF curve: `ett_hourly` peaks at lags 24, 48, 72,
96, 120... (spacing exactly 24, every single gap); `ett_15min` peaks at lags 96, 192,
288 (spacing exactly 96 = 96 × 15 min = 24 hours). Both are the same physical
daily cycle, sampled at different resolutions — real, measured evidence for exactly
the "ETT will likely require seasonal handling" expectation from F0-03, and precisely
the pattern the event-token mechanism (`E_HOUR_PEAK` etc.) is designed to capture.
It is not something a longer context window would resolve — a fixed window can shift
where in the cycle it starts, but can't give the model an absolute clock position,
which is what a recurring calendar-relative pattern actually needs.

**Finance and ett_daily decay cleanly to near-zero**, no periodic re-emergence.
`finance`'s change channel decorrelates almost immediately (lag 10); its volatility
channel takes much longer (lag 137), consistent with the well-known persistence of
volatility clustering. `ett_daily` decorrelates faster on both channels (8, 54) —
plausibly because daily resampling (`.last()` of each calendar day) already discards
the sub-daily seasonal structure that dominates the native-frequency series, leaving
less to decorrelate from in the first place.

## Was the ACF-based recommendation (137/150) contaminated by the rolling-window artifact?

The volatility channel is a 20-period rolling std; overlapping windows sharing up to 19
of 20 observations mechanically induce autocorrelation in *any* input, including pure
noise (verified directly — see `experiments/notes/white_noise_sanity_check.md`'s
follow-up section: raw iid noise has ~zero ACF everywhere, but its rolling(20).std() shows
artificial correlation decaying from 0.94 to ~0 right at lag 19–20). Before trusting
finance's volatility decorrelation lag (137) as genuine, that mechanism needed ruling in
or out as the actual driver.

**Checked directly**, by comparing finance's real volatility ACF against a matched pure-
artifact baseline (iid noise, same total sample size and change-variance, same rolling-20
transform, nothing else):

| lag | pure-artifact ACF (matched noise) | real finance volatility ACF | real − artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.9457 | 0.9872 | 0.0415 |
| 10 | 0.4588 | 0.7945 | 0.3357 |
| 20 | −0.0397 | 0.5229 | 0.5626 |
| 50 | −0.0147 | 0.2713 | 0.2860 |
| 100 | −0.0042 | 0.2014 | 0.2057 |
| **137** | **0.0167** | **0.1951** | **0.1784** |
| 200 | −0.0208 | 0.1231 | 0.1439 |

The pure artifact has already decayed to indistinguishable-from-zero by lag ~20 — matching
the mechanism check exactly. Real finance volatility remains at 0.20 at lag 137, while the
matched artifact contributes essentially nothing (0.017) at that same lag. **The lag that
sets the recommendation is not explained by the windowing mechanism** — by the time you
reach lag 137, the mechanical component has already vanished; what's left is genuine
volatility clustering, a well-documented real phenomenon in finance. The windowing
mechanism does inflate the *early* portion of the curve (lags 1–20), but the number the
recommendation is actually based on sits well past where that inflation has any effect.

## But ACF and PACF disagree sharply on what "137" actually means

ACF measures cumulative/marginal correlation and decays slowly for autoregressive-type
processes *by construction*, even when only a few lags carry genuinely new information —
PACF measures the *direct* contribution of lag *k* after removing what's already explained
by shorter lags, and is the more relevant statistic for "how far back does the model need
to look for new information," not just "how far back is correlation still detectable."

| domain | channel | ACF decorrelation lag | **PACF** decorrelation lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| finance | change | 10 | 10 |
| finance | volatility | 137 | **5** |
| ett_daily | change | 8 | 9 |
| ett_daily | volatility | 54 | **5** |

**The volatility channels' PACF cuts off at lag 5 in both domains**, in sharp contrast to
their 137/54 ACF decorrelation lags. This is the classic signature of a short-memory
autoregressive process whose ACF decays slowly simply because each step propagates a
little of the previous one forward (e.g. an AR(1)-like process has ACF decaying
geometrically forever, but PACF exactly zero after lag 1) — not evidence that lag 100 or
lag 130 individually adds new predictive information beyond what's already captured by
the first several lags. Change channels agree closely between ACF and PACF (10 vs. 10, 8
vs. 9) — no revision needed there.

## Recommended context window: **50** (revised from the provisional 150; was 30 originally)

**PACF, not ACF, is the primary basis for this number.** The largest PACF cutoff across
every clean-decay series is 5 (both volatility channels); 50 is a 10x margin over that —
comfortably beyond the point where PACF says a linear model gains nothing from looking
further back, while leaving real headroom for two things PACF (a linear statistic) can't
see: nonlinear dependence, and whatever an attention-based SLM can exploit that a fitted
AR model can't. 50 is also comfortably past F0-08's finding that lag-10 mutual information
is still substantial (0.49–0.59 bits) — not just barely clearing it.

The 137/150 ACF-only figure is kept as a documented upper-bound reference, not the
recommendation: it reflects real correlation (not a windowing artifact, per the check
above), but that correlation is explained by propagation through much shorter direct
memory, and paying full token cost to chase an indirectly-propagated tail is not
justified once PACF makes that propagation visible. As before, the two oscillating ETT
series are excluded from this calculation for the same reason as previously: a fixed
window can't resolve a ~24-hour seasonal cycle regardless of length, and that's what event
tokens are for.

**Token-budget consequence, on record for F1-05 and F3-06.** At the encoding cost already
established in the backlog (`P_CRASH_V_SURGE` ≈ 5–7 subword tokens under semantic
labelling, ~6.7 tokens/symbol average, i.e. a 30-symbol context ≈ 200 tokens):

| window | semantic encoding (~6.7 tok/symbol) | single-character encoding (~1 tok/symbol) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 (original placeholder) | ~200 tokens | ~30 tokens |
| 50 (this recommendation) | ~335 tokens | ~50 tokens |
| 150 (ACF-only, not used) | ~1000 tokens | ~150 tokens |

50 keeps semantic-label context cost in a comfortable range; 150 would have pushed it
toward roughly a fifth of a 0.5B model's practical context budget on prompt alone, before
any surrounding instruction text. Single-character encoding is cheap at any of these
window sizes — the constraint only bites under semantic labelling. Recorded here so this
isn't rediscovered as a surprise mid-training when F1-05 (encoders) and F3-06 (edge
measurements) are actually built.

**This still differs from the original arbitrary value of 30** (not by the factor-of-5 the
provisional 150 implied, but by a smaller, better-justified margin). `WORKING_PLAN.md`
updated to 50, not 150.
