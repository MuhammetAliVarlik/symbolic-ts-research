# F0-04 — Prototype sigma binning and channel projection

Script: `scripts/prototype_tokenize.py`. Throwaway prototype, not the library — the
point was to surface design questions before locking anything in.

## What it does

For each series (one ETT variant, or one ticker), pick a single "level" reading —
`OT` for ETT, `Adj Close` for finance — and derive two more channels from it:

- `change` = `level.diff()`
- `volatility` = rolling std of `change`, 20-period window

Each of `change` and `volatility` gets its own sigma fit — mean and std computed from
the first 70% of that series only — and is bucketed into 5 neutral labels (`0`..`4`)
using edges at `[-1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5]` standard deviations either side of the
training-fit mean. The two labels combine into one token per timestep, e.g. `C2_V1`.
`level` itself is computed but never tokenised — it's only there to derive `change`.

Ran successfully end to end for all 4 ETT variants and all 7 tickers, and running the
same series through twice produced byte-identical token sequences both times.

## What should change before the real (tested) version

**Raw dollar differences don't hold up over a 12-year window for a growing stock.**
AAPL's mean absolute *daily* price move went from $0.19 in 2014 to $3.63 in 2026 — an
~19x increase — tracking the price itself growing roughly 11x (from ~$20 to ~$231
average). A sigma fit on the training slice ends up calibrated to the *average* dollar
scale across that whole span, so the early, cheap-price years look artificially flat
(the sample output shows dozens of identical `C2_V1` tokens in a row for AAPL and JPM)
while the recent years dominate the tails. That's a scale problem, not a real absence
of movement. **Fix: use percentage or log returns for any price-like level channel**,
not raw absolute differences — this only matters for domains where the level itself
can grow multiplicatively over time (finance); ETT's temperature-like readings don't
have this problem and raw differencing looked fine there.

**The 70/30 split is a placeholder, not real validation.** It's a plain slice, no
purge/embargo gap, no walk-forward folds — it exists here only to prove sigma can be
fit on a subset and applied elsewhere without leakage. The actual splitting logic needs
its own proper implementation.

**The volatility window (20) was picked, not tested.** It matches a familiar
short-window realised-volatility convention, but nothing here compares it against
other window sizes to see whether the resulting buckets are actually more informative.

**Whether `level` should stay untokenised is worth a second look.** It's excluded here
because a non-stationary reading is a bad fit for fixed training-derived thresholds —
sigma computed on one region of a growing series won't describe another region. But
that means the token stream currently carries no absolute-scale information at all,
only shape. Whether that's the right call, or whether some coarse discretised-level
signal is worth adding back in a stationarity-safe way, is still open.
