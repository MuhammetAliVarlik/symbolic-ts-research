# F0-04 — Prototype sigma binning and channel projection

Script: `scripts/prototype_tokenize.py`. Throwaway prototype, not the library — the
point was to surface design questions before locking anything in. This note covers a
follow-up pass after the first version turned up scale problems in both channels.

## What it does (current version)

For each series (one ETT variant, or one ticker), pick a single raw reading — `OT` for
ETT, `Adj Close` for finance — and derive exactly two summary channels from it:

- `change`: log return (`log(level).diff()`) for finance, plain difference
  (`level.diff()`) for ETT.
- `volatility`: rolling std of `change`, 20-period window, bucketed on a log scale.

Each channel gets its own sigma fit — mean and std computed from the first 70% of that
series only — and is bucketed into 5 neutral labels (`0`..`4`) using edges at
`[-1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5]` standard deviations either side of the training-fit mean. The
two labels combine into one token per timestep, e.g. `C2_V1`. There is no `level`
channel in the output at all (see below).

Ran successfully end to end for all 4 ETT variants and all 7 tickers. Running the same
series through twice still produces byte-identical token sequences.

## Fixed in this follow-up

**1. Finance's `change` is now a log return, not a raw dollar difference.**
AAPL's mean absolute *daily* price move went from $0.19 in 2014 to $3.63 in 2026 (~19x),
tracking the price itself growing ~11x. A sigma fit on raw dollar differences ends up
calibrated to whichever price era dominates the training slice, so cheap-price years
look artificially flat. Log returns fix this: post-fix, finance train-fit `change` sigma
sits in a tight, comparable band across all 7 tickers (0.011–0.018), instead of the
wildly different dollar-scale sigmas the first version produced (0.44–3.14). ETT's `OT`
keeps a plain difference — it's a sensor reading with no multiplicative growth, so
there's nothing to fix there.

**2. `level` is now explicitly not a channel — dropped, not silently ignored.**
Only two channels are projected: `change` and `volatility`. A raw level is
non-stationary, so a sigma threshold fit on one region of a growing/drifting series
won't describe another region of the same series — the same failure mode the log-return
fix above addresses, one level up. Cumulative `change` already carries the same
information a coarse discretised level would, without that problem.
**This changes the design from a 3-channel (level, change, volatility) to a 2-channel
(change, volatility) projection — `WORKING_PLAN.md`'s decision on this needs updating to
match.**

**3. Volatility is bucketed on log(volatility), and the sigma fit excludes exact
zeros.** Volatility is non-negative and right-skewed; symmetric sigma bins on the raw
value left the bottom bucket almost empty (1.8% fill, pooled across all 11 series) with
57% of everything landing in bucket 2 alone. Log(volatility) fixes the skew — but ETT's
rolling std also hits exact zero in 0.3–2% of windows (flat sensor stretches), and
log(0) is undefined. That took two fixes, not one:
  - a small epsilon (`log(volatility + 1e-8)`) so classification doesn't crash on zero, and
  - **excluding those same zero-volatility windows from the sigma *fit* itself.**
    The epsilon alone wasn't enough: leaving the epsilon-floor values
    (`log(1e-8) ≈ -18.4`) inside the fit inflated the fitted std by ~4.5x on ETTm1/ETTm2
    (2.23 vs. 0.48, 2.47 vs. 0.54) from just 1.6–2.1% of training rows being that
    extreme — a handful of artificial outliers dominating a variance calculation.
    Excluding them from the fit but still classifying them (they land in bucket 0
    automatically, which is the correct place for genuinely-zero local volatility)
    resolved it. Same sigma mechanism throughout, just applied on the log scale — not a
    switch to quantile bins.

    **Exclusion is tolerance-based (`np.isclose(volatility, 0, atol=1e-10)`), not exact
    equality.** Checked directly: no rolling-std value in either domain lands strictly
    between 0 and 1e-10 — every degenerate window computes as bitwise `0.0`, so the
    tolerance change is a no-op on this data. Kept anyway, defensively: a strict `> 0` /
    `== 0.0` check only happens to work here because these particular flat stretches
    reduce to literal zero; a rolling std over near-constant-but-not-bitwise-identical
    values elsewhere could land on something like `1e-15` and slip through a strict
    check, silently reintroducing the exact fit-inflation problem this exclusion exists
    to prevent.

    **Bucket-fill by domain, not just pooled** (pooling could hide an imbalance in one
    domain by averaging it against the other):

    | domain  | 0    | 1     | 2     | 3     | 4    |
    |---------|------|-------|-------|-------|------|
    | pooled (11 series) | 5.9% | 26.1% | 40.2% | 22.4% | 5.4% |
    | ETT only (4)       | 9.7% | 27.3% | 35.3% | 23.5% | 4.1% |
    | finance only (7)   | 3.7% | 25.4% | 42.9% | 21.8% | 6.2% |

    Both domains individually still land all 5 buckets reasonably filled — this isn't
    the "bucket 0 near-empty" problem from the raw-scale version. But viewed alone, ETT
    is visibly more front-loaded toward bucket 0 (9.7% vs. finance's 3.7%) and lighter
    on bucket 4 (4.1% vs. 6.2%) than the pooled number suggested. That's consistent with
    ETT being the only domain with exact-zero-volatility windows pulling its low end up;
    it isn't a new problem, just one the pooled view was partially averaging out.

    **Zero-volatility fraction, train (first 70%) vs. the remaining 30%:**

    | series | train | remaining 30% |
    |--------|-------|----------------|
    | ETTh1  | 0.33% | 0.31% |
    | ETTh2  | 0.34% | 0.33% |
    | ETTm1  | 1.59% | 1.54% |
    | ETTm2  | 2.11% | 1.67% |
    | finance (all 7) | 0.00% | 0.00% |

    No sign of a regime shift — ETTh1/h2/m1 are within a few percent of each other
    between the two halves, and finance never hits exact zero in either. ETTm2 drops
    from 2.11% to 1.67% (a ~21% relative decrease), the largest gap of the four, but
    it's a small-in-absolute-terms fraction moving a small-in-absolute-terms amount, not
    a jump between regimes. Worth re-checking once the real walk-forward splits (not
    this 70/30 placeholder) exist, but nothing here currently points to instability.

## Still open before the real (tested) version

**The 70/30 split is a placeholder, not real validation.** It's a plain slice, no
purge/embargo gap, no walk-forward folds — it exists here only to prove sigma can be fit
on a subset and applied elsewhere without leakage. The actual splitting logic needs its
own proper implementation.

**The volatility window (20) is being left as-is on purpose.** It matches a familiar
short-window realised-volatility convention, but nothing here compares it against other
window sizes. Revisiting this now would mean picking a number twice before either is
justified — better to wait for the ACF/PACF lag structure and choose the window against
actual autocorrelation, not convention.
