# F0-03 — Stationarity testing across domains

Generated from `results/stationarity.csv` (produced by `scripts/run_stationarity.py`).
Do not hand-edit the numbers below; re-run the script and regenerate this section if the
underlying data or test configuration changes.

## Method

- **ADF** (`statsmodels.tsa.stattools.adfuller`, `autolag="AIC"`, `regression="c"`) —
  null hypothesis: the series has a unit root (non-stationary).
- **KPSS** (`statsmodels.tsa.stattools.kpss`, `regression="c"`, `nlags="auto"`) —
  null hypothesis: the series is (trend-)stationary.
- Both tests run on the **raw** series and on its **first difference**
  (`series.diff().dropna()`) — no log-transform, no seasonal adjustment, no scaling.
  This is deliberately the simplest possible transform so Phase 1's `binning.py`/
  `projection.py` design can be informed by what the raw and once-differenced data
  actually look like, not by an assumed treatment.
- Significance threshold: 5% (`p < 0.05` to reject the null).
- **Channels tested:**
  - ETT: all 7 raw columns (`HUFL, HULL, MUFL, MULL, LUFL, LULL, OT`) x 4 variants
    (`ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2`).
  - Finance: all 6 OHLCV+Adj-Close columns x 7 tickers
    (`AAPL, MSFT, JPM, XOM, JNJ, WMT, SPY`).

## Domain interpretation

**Finance.** Raw price and volume levels are non-stationary under both tests: ADF
rejects the unit root for only 7 of 42 raw series, and KPSS rejects stationarity for 41
of 42. This is the textbook random-walk result for asset prices — and notably, **all
seven ADF exceptions are the `Volume` channel**, one per ticker (`AAPL, JNJ, JPM, MSFT,
SPY, WMT, XOM`). Trading volume mean-reverts around a level; price does not. First
differencing removes the unit root cleanly: ADF finds 100% of differenced series
stationary, KPSS agrees for 41/42 (97.6%). The sole KPSS exception is JPM's differenced
`Adj Close` (p = 0.034, a borderline rejection), while ADF strongly confirms
stationarity for the same series (p ≈ 0) — consistent with volatility clustering rather
than a genuine unit root.

**ETT.** Raw channels show a systematic ADF/KPSS conflict: ADF rejects the unit root
for 27 of 28 raw channels, but KPSS rejects stationarity for all 28. Since both tests
use the same constant-only regression, an ADF-stationary / KPSS-non-stationary
combination is the classic signature of a slowly evolving mean rather than a random
walk — consistent with ETT's known daily/seasonal periodicity, not a unit-root process.
First differencing resolves this cleanly: both tests agree 100% of differenced channels
are stationary. The one raw-level ADF exception (`ETTh2` / `LUFL`, p = 0.13) is
consistent with the same trend/seasonal structure being strong enough there that even
ADF fails to reject.

## Transformation the pipeline will adopt

First-order differencing is the transform that achieves near-unanimous ADF/KPSS
agreement on stationarity in both domains (100%/97.6% for finance, 100%/100% for ETT).
This directly supports decision **D2** (level / change / volatility channel
projection): the "change" channel should be built from first differences, not raw
levels.

The raw-level ADF/KPSS conflict specific to ETT (100% of raw channels vs. finance's
83%) confirms the working plan's stated expectation that "ETT will likely require
seasonal handling." Rather than over-differencing to force away that structure, it is
exactly what decision **D3** (event tokens: `E_WEEKEND`, `E_HOUR_PEAK`, `E_HOLIDAY`,
season-change) is designed to recover downstream — so no additional seasonal
differencing is planned at this stage.
