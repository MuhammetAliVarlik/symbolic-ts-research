# F0-10 — Markov-k baseline, in-domain and cross-domain

Generated by `scripts/run_markov_baseline.py`. Second half of the Phase 0 gate — the
first half was F0-07's transition-matrix similarity test. Answers *"why do you need
500M parameters for next-symbol prediction?"* by setting the bar a transferred SLM must
clear, and tests the gate's other criterion: *"cross-domain Markov performs clearly
worse than in-domain Markov."*

## Method

Orders 1–5, with backoff to lower orders on unseen contexts, down to a Laplace-smoothed
(α=0.5) order-0 base — always available, so perplexity is always finite. `finance` and
`ett_daily` (F0-07's granularity-matched daily-resampled ETT), same choice as every task
since F0-07, for the same reason: lag-for-lag comparability.

**A real bug, not just a design choice, needed fixing first:** the initial run produced
`perplexity = inf` for every single cell. Cause: only *unseen contexts* (per the task
spec) triggered backoff, but a *seen* context observed once or twice still assigned
100% probability to whichever single token happened to follow it and exactly 0% to
everything else — not "confidence," just an inability to represent uncertainty, and
fatal to perplexity the moment a test continuation didn't match. Fixed by applying the
same Laplace smoothing to every order's counts, not just order-0's. This doesn't change
argmax-based accuracy/F1 at all (adding a constant to all counts before normalising
preserves which token has the highest count), only perplexity, which is exactly the
metric that needed it.

Train/test split: first 70% / last 30% of each series — a Phase-0 diagnostic holdout,
**not** the locked test set from D9 (that applies from Phase 4 onward). Evaluation
positions are always the target domain's own test-split positions; context may reach
into that domain's own training tail (legitimate — a real deployed predictor sees a
continuous stream, the train/test split is an evaluation-time artifact, not a real
discontinuity). What changes between in-domain and cross-domain cells is only which
model does the predicting.

**Metrics:** accuracy with Wilson CI (the correct fit — accuracy is a binomial
proportion). Macro-F1 and perplexity with a **bootstrap** CI instead of Wilson — Wilson's
formula is specifically for proportions and doesn't apply to either, so a literal
"Wilson CI" for them would be a misapplication, not a substitution.

## Finding 1: higher Markov orders make things *worse*, not better, in-domain

Accuracy falls monotonically from order 1 to order 5 in **every** cell (finance
in-domain: 0.486→0.408; ett_daily in-domain: 0.454→0.366), and perplexity rises
monotonically in every cell (finance: 4.60→9.28; ett_daily: 5.54→13.03). With a
25-symbol alphabet, order-4/5 contexts are sparse — most are seen only a handful of
times in training (ett_daily's ~1,976 training positions in particular can't support
order-5 contexts from a 25^5 space at all reliably), so the "best" classical Markov
model here is order 1–2, not order 5. This matters for the "why 500M parameters"
framing: the bar isn't the order-5 numbers, it's **~46–49% in-domain accuracy at
order 1–2** — real headroom for an SLM that can exploit the longer-range structure
F0-08 found (mutual information still substantial at lag 10) without needing a sparse
lookup table for every possible 5-symbol context.

## Finding 2: the cross-domain drop is real but not statistically "clear" — and asymmetric

Per the gate's literal criterion, "clearly worse" means a non-overlapping confidence
interval between an in-domain cell and its cross-domain counterpart. Checked precisely,
order by order, in both directions — **the two directions tell opposite stories:**

| order | ett_daily→finance (cross) vs. finance in-domain | finance→ett_daily (cross) vs. ett_daily in-domain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | overlap (barely: [.455,.479] vs. [.474,.498]) | overlap |
| 2 | **no overlap** — cross clearly worse | overlap |
| 3 | **no overlap** — cross clearly worse | overlap |
| 4 | **no overlap** — cross clearly worse | overlap |
| 5 | **no overlap** — cross clearly worse | overlap |

**`ett_daily→finance` is clearly worse than finance's own in-domain baseline at every
order except order 1** — a real, statistically clear cross-domain drop, satisfying the
gate's literal criterion in this direction. **`finance→ett_daily` shows no such drop at
any order** — every cross-domain CI overlaps ett_daily's own in-domain CI, and at order 1
particularly, the cross-domain point estimate (0.461) sits almost on top of the
in-domain one (0.454). The two cross-domain directions are not equivalent, and averaging
them into one number would hide this.

**This asymmetry is at least partly confounded by training-set size, not purely domain
difference.** `ett_daily` has ~1,976 training positions across 4 series vs. finance's
~15,400 across 7 — an order-5 model trained on ett_daily is working with far less data
to begin with, so its *own* in-domain numbers are already somewhat degraded by scarcity
(consistent with Finding 1). Comparing "ett_daily-trained model evaluated on finance's
much larger, more stable test set" against "ett_daily's own small, noisier in-domain
test set" isn't a clean isolation of domain difference alone. This is the same
sample-size theme that's recurred throughout Phase 0 (F0-05's token-count imbalance,
F0-07's granularity mismatch) — flagged honestly rather than glossed over.

## What this means for the Phase 0 gate, taken as a whole

Neither half of the gate passes as a clean, unambiguous "yes" on strict statistical
terms, and neither fails as a clean "no" either:

- **F0-07** (granularity-matched): real, non-artifactual shared structure confirmed
  (white-noise sanity check), but the literal "more similar than chance" test still
  sits at the 96.8th/80.4th percentile of its own null — closer to chance than a
  comfortable pass, not clearly inside it.
- **F0-10** (this task): the gate's literal criterion — cross-domain clearly worse than
  in-domain — is satisfied cleanly in the `ett_daily→finance` direction (non-overlapping
  CIs at orders 2–5), but not satisfied at all in the `finance→ett_daily` direction
  (every order overlaps). One direction passes; the other doesn't. Averaging them into a
  single "cross-domain drop" number would erase a real, directional finding.

**Overall verdict: mixed, leaning toward supporting the transfer hypothesis rather than
against it, but not a clean pass, and asymmetric in a way that itself needs explaining
before Phase 1.** The picture across Phase 0 is consistent with "real shared structure
exists between domains (F0-06/07/08, confirmed non-artifactual), real differences exist
too (this task, at least in one direction), and neither dominates completely" — a more
interesting and more honest claim than either "transfer is trivial" (no drop anywhere,
no reason to expect an SLM to add value) or "transfer is hopeless" (domains share
nothing, Phase 1 needs a redesign). Neither of those clean extremes is what the data
show. What Phase 1 should carry forward is the asymmetry itself, not just an average
degradation: a model direction-agnostic enough to look symmetric in this baseline
(`finance→ett_daily`) but not in the reverse is worth understanding before assuming the
eventual SLM will transfer equally well in both directions — the training-set-size
confound identified above is the leading candidate explanation, but this baseline can't
distinguish it from a genuine directional asymmetry in how much information the two
domains share about each other. Per the working plan's own rule, this mixed result is
reported as found, not resolved into a cleaner story than the numbers support.
