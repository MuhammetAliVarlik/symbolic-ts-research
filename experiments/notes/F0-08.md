# F0-08 — Entropy rate and mutual information

Generated by `scripts/run_information_theory.py`. Run on three token sets: native-
granularity `ett` (hourly/15-min), `finance` (daily), and `ett_daily` (F0-07's
daily-resampled ETT), so results stay directly comparable to F0-06/F0-07.

## Method

**Entropy rate estimator: plug-in (maximum-likelihood) entropy rate under a
first-order Markov assumption** — the empirical-P(from-state)-weighted average of
each row's own Shannon entropy, H = Σₓ P(x)·H(Y\|X=x). Reported alongside the
**Miller-Madow correction** (H_MM = H_plugin + (K−1)/(2·n·ln 2) bits, K = 25 possible
symbols, n = that row's own observation count), applied per row before weighting —
the standard first-order bias correction for plug-in entropy estimates.

**Named bias, and checked, not just asserted:** plug-in entropy estimators are known
to be negatively biased in finite samples — sampling noise makes an estimated
distribution look more peaked (lower entropy) than the true one, worse for smaller
per-row sample sizes. This shows up exactly as expected in the results: `ett_daily`
(2,824 total tokens, the smallest sample of the three) has a bias of 0.1473 bits,
roughly 6x `finance`'s 0.0181 bits and 60x `ett`'s 0.0025 bits — the bias tracks
sample size in the expected direction and by a large margin, not a marginal effect.

**Mutual information at lags 1–10**: plug-in estimate from the joint co-occurrence
distribution of tokens *k* steps apart, within a series only (never across a series
boundary). No smoothing needed — the log ratio is only evaluated where the joint
count is already positive, which makes both marginals positive by construction.

**Lag-1 cross-check against F0-06, before extending to lags 2–10:** F0-06 computed
the observation-weighted mean KL(row‖marginal) — mathematically the same quantity as
lag-1 mutual information, by the identity I(X;Y) = Σₓ P(X=x)·KL(P(Y\|X=x)‖P(Y)), but
computed there via row-conditional KL rather than the joint-count method used here.
Checked directly before trusting anything else in this script:

| domain | F0-06 (row-KL method) | this script (joint-count method) | diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| ett | 1.7448 bits | 1.7448 bits | 0.000027 |
| finance | 1.4964 bits | 1.4964 bits | 0.000046 |

Both **match to floating-point tolerance** (the script raises rather than silently
continuing if they hadn't) — two independent computational paths agree, which is
evidence against a bug in either.

## Results

**Entropy rate** (bits; max possible for 25 symbols = log₂25 = 4.6439):

| domain | plug-in | Miller-Madow | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| ett | 1.9678 | 1.9703 | 0.0025 |
| finance | 2.1417 | 2.1598 | 0.0181 |
| ett_daily | 2.2799 | 2.4273 | 0.1473 |

All three sit at roughly 40–52% of the maximum possible entropy — well below "close
to random." There is substantial predictable structure in the symbolic sequences of
every domain and granularity tested, which is the reassuring answer to the question
this task exists to ask (*"if entropy rate is near-maximal, no model will do well"* —
it isn't, so that specific failure mode is ruled out here).

**Mutual information, lags 1–10** (bits; `results/information_theory.csv`):

| lag | ett | finance | ett_daily |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.745 | 1.496 | 1.588 |
| 2 | 1.468 | 1.245 | 1.299 |
| 3 | 1.270 | 1.081 | 1.078 |
| 4 | 1.111 | 0.949 | 0.912 |
| 5 | 0.982 | 0.841 | 0.789 |
| 6 | 0.879 | 0.750 | 0.699 |
| 7 | 0.791 | 0.670 | 0.629 |
| 8 | 0.716 | 0.605 | 0.564 |
| 9 | 0.651 | 0.546 | 0.533 |
| 10 | 0.594 | 0.491 | 0.488 |

Mutual information decays smoothly and monotonically with lag in all three domains/
granularities — no lag shows an unexpected jump, which would have suggested a bug or
an artificial periodicity from the tokenisation itself. Still clearly nonzero at lag
10 in every case (0.49–0.59 bits, roughly a third of the lag-1 value) — memory extends
past a handful of steps, relevant context for F0-09's context-window justification.

**A second, independent confirmation of F0-07's granularity-matching diagnostic.**
`finance` and `ett_daily`'s MI curves sit far closer to each other than native `ett`
does to either, at every lag from 3 to 10: lag 3, 1.081 vs. 1.078 bits (0.2%
relative); lag 10, 0.491 vs. 0.488 bits (0.6% relative) — the exact pair the
diagnostic pointed at. It isn't a perfectly flat match throughout: lags 4–8 show a
small but real gap (0.037–0.051 bits, `finance` running slightly higher), and lags 1–2
actually invert (`ett_daily` above `finance` by 0.09 and 0.05 bits) before the curves
cross around lag 3. But this whole finance-vs-ett_daily range (−0.09 to +0.05 bits) is
still much smaller than native `ett`'s gap over `finance`, which stays positive and
substantial at every single lag through 10 (0.104–0.248 bits, shrinking steadily but
never crossing zero). Native `ett` is the outlier here, not `finance` or `ett_daily`.

This matters because it's a completely different statistic from F0-07's (MI decay
*shape* here, vs. Frobenius norm/KL *distance* between transition matrices there)
landing on the same conclusion: once sampling frequency is equalized, not just the
transition structure (F0-07) but also the memory-decay profile (this task) becomes
comparable between domains. Two independent statistics agreeing is stronger evidence
than either alone, and both should be cited together in whatever synthesis note
eventually closes out Phase 0.
