# F0-07 — Cross-domain transition similarity (Phase 0 gate)

Generated by `scripts/run_transition_similarity.py`. This computes one half of the
Phase 0 gate: *"transition matrices of the two domains are measurably more similar
than chance."* (The other half — cross-domain Markov clearly worse than in-domain
Markov — is F0-10's job, not run yet.)

## Method

**Two statistics**, both computed on the real matrices and on a permutation null:

- **Frobenius norm** of the matrix difference, on the unsmoothed F0-06 matrices
  (explicit zeros are fine here — no log anywhere).
- **Row-wise KL divergence.** Checked directly before computing anything: naive KL on
  the F0-06 explicit-zero matrices is unusable — 22 of 23 commonly-defined rows have
  KL(ett‖finance) = +∞ (finance has a hard zero somewhere ETT doesn't), and 10 of 23
  have KL(finance‖ett) = +∞ (the reverse). **Jeffreys smoothing** (α = 0.5 pseudocount
  per cell) is applied for this comparison only — it does not touch the F0-06 matrices
  on disk, which correctly stay explicit-zero for their own purpose. Reported as the
  symmetrised mean, (KL(ett‖finance) + KL(finance‖ett)) / 2.

Both statistics are restricted to the 23/25 rows defined in *both* domains — excluding
`C0_V0` and `C4_V0` (finance has zero observations there, per F0-06), since comparing a
real distribution against "no data" isn't a comparison of structure.

**Null:** ≥1000 permutations, each independently shuffling every series' token order
*within* that series (both domains, series boundaries respected). This destroys
sequential/conditional structure while leaving each domain's marginal essentially
untouched — token counts are exactly preserved; only which token happens to land last
in a given series (and is therefore excluded from being a "from" state) can shift by
±1 per series, negligible against totals in the thousands to hundreds of thousands.
Both statistics recomputed on the shuffled pair, 1000 times, to build the null.

## Result

| statistic | observed | null mean (sd) | percentile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frobenius norm | **1.3277** | 0.5957 (0.0301) | **100.0** |
| symmetrised KL | **0.3761 bits** | 0.1735 (0.0106) | **100.0** |

The observed value sits about 24 standard deviations above the null mean on both
statistics (visible directly in Figure A7's histogram — the observed line doesn't even
approach the null cluster). Percentile 100 here means the real ETT/finance transition
matrices are *more different* from each other than **every single one** of 1000
chance replicates — the opposite of the gate's literal criterion, which needs the real
matrices to be *more similar* than chance.

## Where the divergence comes from (not evenly spread)

Per-row Frobenius contribution (`results/transition_similarity_row_detail.csv`) is
concentrated in the rows with the largest change and/or volatility labels:

| from_token | ett n | finance n | row Frobenius |
|---|---|---|---|
| C4_V3 | 6073 | 396 | 0.549 (largest) |
| C4_V4 | 1602 | 233 | 0.526 |
| C4_V2 | 2678 | 270 | 0.395 |
| C1_V4 | 2503 | 233 | 0.387 |
| C0_V4 | 1477 | 234 | 0.372 |
| ⋮ | | | |
| C2_V1 | 32877 | 3788 | 0.057 (smallest) |
| C2_V2 | 29464 | 4977 | 0.097 |

The rows dominated by extreme change and/or high volatility (`0`/`4` on either axis)
drive most of the total divergence. The highest-sample, most typical rows (`C2_V1`,
`C2_V2` — small change, moderate volatility) are the *best* aligned across domains,
visible in Figure A7's difference heatmap as the lightest cells near the centre, with
the sharp red/blue cells sitting at the extremes.

**A genuine mitigating signal, checked independently:** pooling all 23×25 cells,
`(ett_row - ett_marginal)` and `(finance_row - finance_marginal)` correlate at
**r = 0.855** — strongly positive. Concretely, in most rows both domains' *biggest*
deviation from their own marginal points at the *same* target token (e.g. both domains'
`C1_V0` row deviates most toward `C2_V0`; both `C1_V1` rows deviate most toward
`C2_V1`). The domains agree on the *qualitative* shape of their conditional structure —
self-persistence toward moderate states — but not on its *magnitude*, and Frobenius/KL
measure absolute difference, not correlation of pattern. A shared direction with
mismatched scale still produces a large absolute distance.

## Verdict

**This half of the Phase 0 gate fails as literally specified.** The observed
cross-domain difference is not smaller than chance — it's larger than 100% of 1000
permutation replicates, on both statistics. Per the working plan: *"if similarity is
absent: stop, revisit binning/projection, do not start training."* This is exactly
that situation, not a borderline call.

Two things temper how alarming this should be, without overturning the result:

1. The divergence is concentrated in rare, extreme-event rows (large |change| and/or
   high volatility) — exactly the rows F0-06 already flagged as having the fewest
   observations even where "sufficient" by the 30-row floor. The typical, high-sample
   rows that actually carry most of the sequence's probability mass are comparatively
   well aligned.
2. The domains' deviations from their own marginals correlate strongly in direction
   (r = 0.855) even though they differ in magnitude — there is a shared qualitative
   pattern (self-persistence toward moderate change/volatility), just not one that
   survives an absolute-distance test at the current binning resolution.

This does not by itself justify continuing to Phase 1. Per the working plan, that
decision needs F0-10's cross-domain-vs-in-domain Markov comparison as the second half
of the gate before a final call — a Markov model transferring reasonably despite this
result (e.g., if the transferable signal lives mostly in the well-aligned high-sample
rows, and extreme-event rows contribute little to overall predictive accuracy because
they're rare) would be informative in a different direction than this test. Recorded
here honestly rather than reframed, per the project's own rule that a negative result
completing a phase is not a lesser outcome than a positive one.
