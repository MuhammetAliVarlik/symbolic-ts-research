# F0-05 — Token frequency distributions

Generated from `results/token_frequency.csv` (produced by
`scripts/run_token_frequency.py`), using the F0-04 tokenisation as-is: 2-channel
(change, volatility) projection, sigma bins fit per series on its own first 70%,
pooled across all 4 ETT variants and all 7 finance tickers into one distribution per
domain.

## Method

25 possible tokens (`C0_V0` .. `C4_V4`, 5 change buckets x 5 volatility buckets).
Compared with:

- **Chi-square test of independence** on the 2x25 domain-by-token contingency table.
- **Cramér's V** as an effect size alongside it, since chi-square alone is misleading
  at this sample size (see below).
- **Jensen-Shannon divergence** (base 2, bounded [0, 1]) between the two proportion
  distributions — symmetric, doesn't need a null distribution to interpret.

## Results

- Chi-square: χ² = 2287.75, dof = 24, p ≈ 0. Technically "significant," but with
  174,120 ETT tokens and 22,022 finance tokens pooled, chi-square will find almost any
  real difference significant regardless of how small it is — the p-value alone isn't
  informative here.
- **Cramér's V = 0.108** — a small effect by the usual convention (< 0.1 negligible,
  0.1–0.3 small). Domain explains very little of which token appears.
- **Jensen-Shannon divergence = 0.023** (out of a maximum of 1) — the two distributions
  are close in shape.
- ETT uses all 25/25 tokens at least once. Finance uses 23/25 — the two missing tokens
  are `C0_V0` and `C4_V0`: an extreme change (bucket 0 or 4, beyond 1.5 training-fit σ)
  paired with the *lowest* volatility bucket. That combination essentially never
  happens in finance, and it shouldn't: large price moves cluster with high, not low,
  volatility (the well-known volatility-clustering pattern GARCH models are built
  around). This is a small, explicable gap, not evidence of a fragmented vocabulary.
- Top-5 tokens by share: ETT `{C2_V1, C2_V2, C1_V2, C2_V0, C3_V2}` (60.5% of all ETT
  tokens), finance `{C2_V2, C2_V1, C3_V2, C1_V2, C2_V3}` (65.4% of all finance tokens).
  **4 of the top 5 are shared between domains** (`C2_V1, C2_V2, C1_V2, C3_V2`); neither
  domain concentrates in only 2–3 symbols, and neither spreads uniformly across all 25
  (uniform would put every token at 4%, and top-5 would be ~20%) — both sit in between,
  and in the same handful of tokens.

## Verdict: comparable enough to proceed

Neither warning sign in the acceptance criteria shows up: no domain concentrates in a
tiny handful of symbols, and the domains are not just nominally sharing a vocabulary —
they visibly favour the same tokens for the same reason (both change and volatility
sigma bins are, by construction, centred on "typical" behaviour, and "typical" for both
a sensor reading and a stock price is a small change with moderate volatility). The
measurable difference between domains is real (Jensen-Shannon divergence is not zero,
and finance never uses the 2 extreme-move/zero-volatility tokens) but small in size and
has a specific, understandable cause rather than looking like two unrelated
vocabularies that happen to share symbol names. This clears the F0-05 gate; proceeding
to the transition-matrix comparison (F0-06) using this token scheme unchanged.

## Flag for later: token-count imbalance across domains (no action needed now)

ETT's pooled token count (174,120) is ~8x finance's (22,022). This is entirely a
sampling-frequency artifact — ETT is hourly (`ETTh1/h2`) or 15-minute (`ETTm1/m2`)
against finance's daily bars — not anything about the tokenisation or vocabulary
itself, and it doesn't affect today's comparisons since everything above is compared as
within-domain proportions, not raw counts.

It will matter once training mixes both domains in one run (T3: finance + ETT training
data, generalising to a third domain) — naively pooling sequences would let ETT
dominate the training signal by sheer sequence count, independent of whether ETT is
actually more informative. Needs a resampling or loss-weighting strategy when that
phase starts. Noting it here now so it doesn't get rediscovered from scratch later.
